I made this water bladder hose for my 2026 Great Divide Mountain Bike Route ride. Ever on a budget and skeptical of "big gear," I didn't want to buy in to one of the proprietary water bladder systems made by Osprey, Camelbak, MSR, etc – especially considering that every day liquid container threads are already highly standardized.

The idea was to utilize the common 28mm thread found on most plastic water bottles to make a cheap hydration bladder hose that was A. long enough to work on a bike, B. less than $10, and C. made with common hardware store parts. Some companies do make this exact product, but they are built on plastic injection molded parts which are irreplaceable and not modular.

I used the standard 3/8" OD vinyl tubing. The concept boils down to a "bulkhead" fitting with hoses connected on either side. I tested a few different pipe fittings, including quick-connect plastic fittings (too leaky) and hose barb connectors (not tight enough). I settled on compression fittings with nylon (as opposed to brass) compression sleeves. These held the tubing super tightly without deforming or perforating it, and the system had no leaks. It was the most hydrated I've ever been while building something.

The system lasted the entire 2,000-mile, 37-day bike ride, and in fact outlived my Sawyer water bladder which I had to replace. I trust it will last many more miles, too. Mission success.
