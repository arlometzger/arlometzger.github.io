    description: "A ball vise is one of the most useful tools for hand engraving, jewelery making, and other fine metal crafts. It swivels on a round base so the user can work on a piece from all angles, and can hold almost any shape with an array of pins. These vises can cost upwards of $1000 new, so I designed and machined this one over a semester-long machine shop course in my senior year of college. It rotates on two bearings, has a cross-threaded bolt to close symetrically, and has a spring-loaded locking set screw. Overall, the designing and machining took over 300 hours."

  - title: "Turning the round base"
    description: "I made the round base by cutting a series of angles into a cylindrical workpiece, then filing the resulting edges down until it matched a template I prepared. I then sanded and polished it to a mirror finish, so that it glides smoothly when rotated. In an ideal design, the radius of the base would position the workpiece to always be at or near the center of rotation."

  - title: "Driving the vise"
    descriptin: "The vise features a bolt with left-hand threads on one side, and right-hand threads on the other. This screws into corresponging threads in the vise's jaws, allowing them to open or close symetrically as the bolt is turned. I filed a square hole into the ends of the bolt, and made a matching square driver tool to turn it and apply significant clamping force when necessary."
